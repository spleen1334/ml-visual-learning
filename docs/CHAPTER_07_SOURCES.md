- PDF pages 84–85: supervised learning uses an observed label to predict a label or value; classification predicts classes for categorical variables. Examples include churn, customer segments and sentiment. Page 85 emphasizes that QA and profiling come first.

## Adaptation and corrections

The chapter stays at orientation level. Its prominent definition combines the source's supervised-label framing with the classification framing: learn from labeled examples to predict a category for a new case. The CRM churn example is a small teaching illustration based on the course's later classification example; its output shows the possible categories “Yes or No”, rather than inventing a fitted prediction from the displayed few rows. The use case says predictions can inform outreach review, not explain or cause churn.

The roadmap follows the PDF's three headings and lists the VTT preview topics. Chapter 8, 9 and 10 remain separate future chapters; no lessons or methods from them are taught here.
