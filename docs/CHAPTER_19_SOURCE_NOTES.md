## Workbook details and boundaries

- `Apriori` used range is `B2:BC9535`. Original transactions are rows 4–9534, IDs 1–9531, date/time plus up to five product columns (`E:I`). The source workbook's filtered area applies the .04 minimum support using each transaction's least frequent item. It retains 5,238 IDs. The unique-item frequency table has 94 items, ranked mostly by frequency: Coffee (4,706; .493757), Bread (3,084; .323576), Tea (1,343; .140909), Cake (967; .101458), Pastry (808; .084776), Sandwich (671; .070402), Medialuna (582; .061064), Hot chocolate (547; .057392), Cookies (503; .052775), Brownie (370; .038821), etc. Preserve workbook order exactly: Scone (321; .033680) appears before Soup (323; .033889) in the source list. The worksheet chart `Support (Top 50 Products)` is a bar chart over `V4:V53` and support values `X4:X53`; the full 94-item list is in `V4:X97`. At .04, Brownie and lower fall below the cutoff. The lesson keeps the top-50 chart plus a scrollable full frequency table.
- Rows `AE4:AF4` store threshold and filtered-row count. Selected items are at `AE9`/`AE12`; supports are `AH5:AH7`, confidence `AH9`, lift `AH11`. Default selected pair is Coffee and Tea; its cached joint support is .050210, confidence .101690 and lift .721671. Formula caveat: single-item supports are counts divided by all 9,531 transactions (`W/COUNT(B4:B9534)`), while joint support is filtered co-occurrence count divided by 5,238 (`SUM(BC)/AF4`). The Coffee/Tea example therefore uses inconsistent denominators and should be labeled as the worksheet's output rather than a coherent single support calculation. The video also manually inspects Coffee/Bread, Coffee/Cake (lift about 1.14) and Coffee/Pastry (lift about 1.54); retain these as reported source results, not independently harmonized metrics.
- `Markov Chain` used range is `B1:AH14421`; 14,418 customer records occupy rows 4–14421. Transition categories are Bike, Bike basket, Bike speaker, Tent, Backpack, Cookware, Cooler, and `-` (no recorded second purchase). Matrix rows and columns are ordered exactly as listed. The workbook's lower probability block divides each row's frequency by that row total. Selector default `K24` is Bike and formula result `O24` is Bike speaker. The sheet also contains two scatter chart objects titled `Full` with helper ranges `Y4:Z2002`; the lesson reproduces the more interpretable matrix-driven outcome chart from the source transition probabilities and preserves every matrix cell. Use the worksheet’s full matrix rather than treating its output as a trained predictive model.
- The PDF's Markov chain matrix contains a complete universe of subscription states; the workbook's purchase-path matrix has a “no second purchase” outcome. Keep these separate examples. The workbook uses a dash for none, not an observed product.
- Source language sometimes overstates a lift > 1 as a “real” association. Present lift > 1 as positive association relative to independence in this sample; minimum support, sample scope and domain context still matter. Association alone does not establish causation or guaranteed recommendation value.
- The PDF describes a .15 illustration on the 20-row bacon/eggs table, while the Coastal Roasters workbook uses .04 on its own 9,531 transactions. Keep each threshold attached to its example.
