## Fidelity and correction boundaries

- Lesson 9.20 replaces the static p.157 surface with a rotatable SVG projection of a two-input logistic surface. It preserves X₁/X₂, probability height from 0 to 1, and the turquoise-to-yellow encoding. Since the source supplies no surface coefficients or numeric input scales, the recreation explicitly uses illustrative β₀=0, β₁=β₂=1 with internal input ranges −3 to 3; it does not claim an exact fitted-source reconstruction. Rotation/elevation change only the view, not the probabilities.

- Preserve the binary observed labels at 0/1, the probability curve, its unit-labelled input axis, the 0.5 reference, and the case-study data/table separately from the synthetic spam/quit illustrations. Keep probability distinct from a class prediction: a threshold is a decision rule, and the source's threshold examples depend on the relative cost of errors.
- The PDF/subtitles describe maximizing the likelihood across observations. At the observation level the stated formula is sound. Across independent observations, the joint likelihood is the **product** of each `Lᵢ` (or the log-likelihood is the sum of `log Lᵢ`). The saved workbook instead has `W6=SUMIF(U4:U627,"<>#NUM!")` = `594.43112022596176`, a sum of per-row observed-class probabilities. It labels this “LIKELIHOOD” and uses it as the displayed objective; this is not the joint likelihood or log-likelihood and should not be described as either. Treat the number as the worksheet's score if reproducing it, or use the mathematically correct objective and label that as a correction.
- The workbook's E/F cached predictions and the graph's S/U values are distinct calculation paths: E/F use W4/W5; S/U use I4/I6. The saved state aligns these, but turning off Optimize can separate them. A demonstration should make its selected coefficients and objective explicit and avoid implying that this workbook snapshot trains a model live.
- The course informally describes likelihood as a function of distance from a point to the curve. The exact Bernoulli likelihood depends on predicted probability and observed 0/1, not geometric distance. Keep the curve-distance description as visual intuition only.
- Lesson 9.21 calls its email data manufactured; don't infer a causal email effect or generalize its suggested weekly maximum from this example. Distinguish the model probability from a business action threshold. The source's “maximum likelihood” coefficient values and workbook score come from the saved exercise state; the workbook alone does not document a reproducible optimizer setup.
