## Workbook evidence and implementation guidance

The workbook sheet `Sentiment Analysis` has dimension `B1:AL66`. Training reviews occupy rows 4–50 (47 observations): raw review in B, supplied cleaned text in D, 25 binary word-presence columns G:AE, hand-scored sentiment in AG (0 negative, 1 positive), logistic prediction in AI, and likelihood-related values in AJ. The test section begins at row 55; seven unseen reviews are in rows 57–63, with raw text in B, cleaned text in D, actual hand-scored comparison label in AG, and model probability in AI. The feature vocabulary includes `terrible`, `no`, `avoid`, `struggle`, `never`, `love`, `hate`, `good`, `bad`, `great`, `wouldn't`, `don't`, `waste`, `trash`, `favorite`, `alright`, `nothing`, `like`, `devoid`, `garbage`, `best`, `crap`, `why`, `worse`, and `better`. The workbook’s Solver objective shown at AJ52 is about 45.5; values are workbook-specific and depend on its included formulas/weight cells. Do not imply the course’s small review set is representative.

For a compact interactive lesson, use a new small, explicitly illustrative review set and visibly compute token flags from the selected cleaned review. Explain that a 1 means the token occurs at least once; bag-of-words drops order and most context. A cleaning toggle should preserve negation terms such as “not” when used, since removing them can reverse sentiment. If showing classifier probabilities or coefficients without extracting and reproducing the actual fitted workbook model, label them illustrative; do not present hand-picked weights as learned results. Keep raw review, cleaned review, tokens, label, and prediction distinct.

## Source corrections and boundaries

- The VTT labels the story “Mark Twain, The Datadog,” while the case video repeatedly says “Bark Twain the Data Dog”; retain one clear fictional case title consistently.
- The workbook test section includes true labels for its seven reviews, so those are evaluation labels, not inputs available during prediction. Reveal them as a separate comparison after predictions.
- Sentiment labels are subjective human annotations; explain that labeling guidance and agreement matter. Do not treat the course’s manually assigned labels as objective truth.
- Binary word presence ignores frequency, word order, syntax, scope of negation, sarcasm, and many forms of context. Cleaning is task-dependent; indiscriminate stop-word removal can discard useful signals such as negation.
- The rendered equation on p.142 is the standard sigmoid, `1 / (1 + e^−(β₀ + β₁x))`. The likelihood visual on p.149 defines high likelihood for a prediction near the observed class and low likelihood for a mismatch. Precisely, binary logistic likelihood is the product of per-observation probabilities assigned to each observed class (usually optimized as summed log-likelihood); use that definition if reproducing the optimization lesson.
